## Question and candidate sources

Determine whether two independent public studies supply usable quantitative
constraints on processing rather than another list of detected peptides:

1. Sahu et al., Nature Communications 2021, DOI10.1038/s41467-021-26427-0,
   PXD018711/PXD018722/PXD028912. Publisher metadata describes defined human
   cyclin-B1/ubiquitin processing by 20S/26S, cellular machinery perturbation and
   proteasome-trapped peptides. Eleven supplementary workbooks and source data
   are linked publicly. Capture, whole-cell, biochemical and tryptic composition
   assays must remain separate. Preserve branched forms and constructs without
   flattening them into canonical linear peptides. No microbial reporter is a
   prediction target in this human-substrate study.
2. Liepe et al., eLife2015, DOI10.7554/eLife.07545; original Dryad
   DOI10.5061/dryad.nk453. A small workbook contains fluorogenic substrate kinetics
   for mouse and human standard/immunoproteasomes. Check units, initial amounts,
   calibration, replicate grain, time and machinery context. Fluorophore release
   is a distinct assay; it cannot become natural peptide-fragment concentration
   or sequence-diverse availability supervision by renaming its columns.

These are candidates, not admitted training sets. Published biological findings
have been read. No claims of blind study discovery or predictive validation.
The first source tests whether broader product/perturbation data exist; the second
tests independent quantitative kinetic constraints. Neither replaces APC data.
